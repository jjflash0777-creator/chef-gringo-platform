## Core caption

Most cooks learn mirepoix as a ratio: 2 parts onion, 1 part carrot, 1 part celery.

Useful? Absolutely. The whole story? Not even close.

The name reaches back to an eighteenth-century French duke. Early preparations called mirepoix could contain meat, fat, herbs, wine and stock. Even the familiar vegetable-only 2:1:1 formula is a later professional shorthand—not a culinary commandment.

The better lesson is what the vegetables are doing.

Onion brings sulfur-driven aroma that changes with heat. Carrot adds sweetness and a complex terpene profile. Celery contributes potent aromatics—including phthalides that researchers have shown can change how complex and savory a broth is perceived to be.

Then your knife and your heat decide what happens next.

Short cook? Smaller cut.  
Long stock? Larger cut.  
Delicate base? Sweat it.  
Brown stock or braise? Color may be exactly what you want.

The goal is not to memorize mirepoix.

**Understand what it is doing, and you can build flavor without the recipe.**
