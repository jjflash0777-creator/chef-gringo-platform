# IDDSI Pilot Claim Audit

## Scope and method

This audit records the seeded state of the twelve-step pilot before editorial revision. The baseline is commit `d27494e6b2a927153d6074c842667a153b03bba4`. The seed is explicitly draft material; nothing below is a clinical direction, a resident-specific order, or a claim of IDDSI conformity.

Claim classes:

- **Evidence** — factual or procedural content that needs an external source.
- **Operational judgment** — a local process choice that may be reasonable but is not universal.
- **Facility policy** — a control that must be supplied by the operating facility or governing jurisdiction.
- **Clinical boundary** — a decision that must remain with an appropriately qualified clinician or interdisciplinary team.

## Step-by-step audit

### 1. Confirm the prescribed texture requirement

- **Existing instruction:** Confirm the individual’s current prescribed texture requirement and facility-approved instructions before production.
- **Purpose:** Prevent work against an assumed, outdated, or mismatched requirement.
- **Expected result:** One current resident-specific requirement and escalation contact.
- **Measurable check:** Record the instruction source, date, resident/menu identifier, and confirmer.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Reliance on memory, prior instructions, or a tray ticket without checking the current approved record.
- **Corrective action:** Stop the individual item and use the facility escalation process.
- **Evidence claims:** A therapeutic diet is resident-specific and must be prescribed or otherwise authorized under applicable rules; food must meet individual needs.
- **Operational judgment:** Which production record is checked first.
- **Facility policy:** Authoritative order location, approved identifiers, escalation chain, and documentation fields.
- **Clinical boundary:** Selecting, interpreting, or changing the prescribed texture.

### 2. Review the menu item and production quantity

- **Existing instruction:** Confirm menu item, recipe, allergens, yield, portions, and batch size from approved records.
- **Purpose:** Align production with meal service and resident-specific requirements.
- **Expected result:** A controlled method and quantity target.
- **Measurable check:** Compare planned portions and yield with the production sheet.
- **Risk:** Medium.
- **Common mistake:** Visual scaling or combining incompatible components.
- **Corrective action:** Recalculate from the approved recipe or obtain supervisory review.
- **Evidence claims:** Menu items and recipes must be consistently prepared and accommodate allergies; IDDSI results can vary with ingredients, equipment, temperature, and holding.
- **Operational judgment:** Batch size and production sequencing.
- **Facility policy:** Approved recipe, allergen matrix, menu substitution, yield, and batch controls.
- **Clinical boundary:** Whether a resident may receive a substitute.

### 3. Select an appropriate preparation method

- **Existing instruction:** Choose an approved method and equipment suited to the food, batch, and intended result.
- **Purpose:** Match process and equipment without implying equipment guarantees conformity.
- **Expected result:** A documented method and equipment choice.
- **Measurable check:** Record method, pre-processing, and batch limits.
- **Risk:** Medium.
- **Common mistake:** Choosing by availability or overloading equipment.
- **Corrective action:** Reduce batch size or use an approved alternative.
- **Evidence claims:** Product, ingredient, temperature, equipment, skill, and holding variables affect the result.
- **Operational judgment:** Equipment selection and practical batch capacity.
- **Facility policy:** Approved equipment, recipes, batch limits, and operator competency.
- **Clinical boundary:** None if the prescribed level is not altered.

### 4. Prepare and cook the food safely

- **Existing instruction:** Use approved recipe, sanitation, allergen, and food-safety procedures.
- **Purpose:** Establish a controlled base before texture processing.
- **Expected result:** Ingredients prepared and cooked under approved controls.
- **Measurable check:** Complete required preparation and cooking records.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Treating purée processing as a substitute for cooking or allergen controls.
- **Corrective action:** Stop, correct if allowed, or discard and restart.
- **Evidence claims:** Food must be prepared and served under professional food-safety standards; cooking, contamination prevention, and allergen controls are distinct from texture modification.
- **Operational judgment:** Production sequence.
- **Facility policy:** Adopted jurisdictional temperatures, recipes, monitoring, allergen controls, and disposition rules.
- **Clinical boundary:** Resident-specific allergy and intolerance instructions.

### 5. Process the food into a smooth purée

- **Existing instruction:** Process controlled batches to a smooth, uniform result without making an unsourced conformity claim.
- **Purpose:** Create a consistent base for assessment.
- **Expected result:** A uniform batch ready for testing.
- **Measurable check:** Inspect the full batch for inconsistency.
- **Risk:** Medium.
- **Common mistake:** Processing only the top or overloading equipment.
- **Corrective action:** Reprocess smaller batches and recombine only when approved.
- **Evidence claims:** Level 4 puréed food has defined texture characteristics and requires applicable IDDSI tests.
- **Operational judgment:** Batch size, processing time, and equipment technique.
- **Facility policy:** Controlled recipe, equipment instructions, and recombination rules.
- **Clinical boundary:** None unless recipe alteration affects the prescribed plan.

### 6. Adjust consistency without reducing safety or quality

- **Existing instruction:** Use only approved ingredients and methods; record additions and avoid unsupported substitutions.
- **Purpose:** Correct consistency while preserving recipe, allergen, nutrition, and quality controls.
- **Expected result:** Traceable adjustments followed by reassessment.
- **Measurable check:** Record each addition and reassess the affected batch.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Unmeasured liquid or thickener, or ingredient changes without checking restrictions.
- **Corrective action:** Stop, quantify, seek approved guidance, and remake when recovery is uncertain.
- **Evidence claims:** Recipe variables affect texture-test results; food must accommodate resident allergies and individual dietary needs.
- **Operational judgment:** The smallest practical adjustment within an approved recipe.
- **Facility policy:** Permitted ingredients, quantities, nutrition/allergen review, recovery, and discard rules.
- **Clinical boundary:** Changes that affect therapeutic diet, hydration, nutrition, medication interaction, or swallowing plan.

### 7. Conduct the applicable texture assessment

- **Existing instruction:** Use a current approved assessment supported by a verified source; do not improvise criteria.
- **Purpose:** Check the result against the applicable requirement.
- **Expected result:** A documented pass/fail result at the relevant service condition.
- **Measurable check:** Record method, condition, result, time, and tester.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Remembered criteria, unrepresentative samples, or unsupported pass records.
- **Corrective action:** Treat as failed or unverified, isolate, correct, and retest.
- **Evidence claims:** Level 4 puréed food is evaluated with the IDDSI Fork Drip and Spoon Tilt tests; testing conditions can affect results.
- **Operational judgment:** Representative sampling plan.
- **Facility policy:** Test form, sampling, tester competency, service-condition definition, and failed-test disposition.
- **Clinical boundary:** Determining the resident’s required level or authorizing deviation.

### 8. Correct a failed result

- **Existing instruction:** Isolate, identify the failure, use only approved correction, and repeat the full assessment.
- **Purpose:** Prevent failed or uncertain product from reaching service.
- **Expected result:** Documented pass after correction or withheld product.
- **Measurable check:** Record failure, correction, retest, and disposition.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Guesswork, testing only the adjusted portion, or serving after an undocumented retest.
- **Corrective action:** Withhold, correct and retest, or discard/escalate.
- **Evidence claims:** Conformity cannot be inferred after a change; applicable tests must be performed on the service-ready result.
- **Operational judgment:** Whether an approved recipe permits recovery.
- **Facility policy:** Isolation, correction, retest, discard, notification, and documentation.
- **Clinical boundary:** Any request to serve a nonconforming item or alter the prescribed requirement.

### 9. Portion and plate consistently

- **Existing instruction:** Portion a verified batch while preventing cross-contact, separation, and identification errors.
- **Purpose:** Preserve controls through resident-specific service.
- **Expected result:** Consistent, identifiable, protected portions.
- **Measurable check:** Compare counts and identifiers with production/service records.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Mixing batches, losing identification, or allowing texture to change.
- **Corrective action:** Stop affected portions; reliably restore identity or remake.
- **Evidence claims:** Food must meet individual needs and allergies; texture can vary with holding and service conditions.
- **Operational judgment:** Plating sequence and presentation.
- **Facility policy:** Portion tools, resident identification, allergen separation, transport, holding, and recheck triggers.
- **Clinical boundary:** Substitution or service where identity/order is uncertain.

### 10. Verify safe service temperature

- **Existing instruction:** Measure and document temperature under current facility policy and applicable guidance; use no unsourced threshold.
- **Purpose:** Control and document service conditions.
- **Expected result:** A documented result that meets the locally applicable requirement or a withheld item.
- **Measurable check:** Record measurement, time, equipment, and corrective action.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Inferring temperature from equipment or appearance.
- **Corrective action:** Withhold, correct, remeasure, and document.
- **Evidence claims:** Food must be served at a safe and appetizing temperature; time/temperature controls depend on food, process, and adopted requirements.
- **Operational judgment:** Measurement point and service sequencing.
- **Facility policy:** Adopted limits, calibrated device, measurement method, corrective actions, and records.
- **Clinical boundary:** Resident-specific temperature restrictions when present.

### 11. Document completion or corrective action

- **Existing instruction:** Complete production, assessment, correction, verification, and disposition records.
- **Purpose:** Preserve operational traceability.
- **Expected result:** A record of what was produced, checked, corrected, and released or withheld.
- **Measurable check:** Review for missing fields and unresolved failures.
- **Risk:** Medium.
- **Common mistake:** Recording only passes or omitting correction reasons.
- **Corrective action:** Reconcile while evidence is available and escalate uncertainty.
- **Evidence claims:** The exact record set is not universal; documentation is a facility control that supports consistent execution and review.
- **Operational judgment:** Workflow sign-off sequence.
- **Facility policy:** Required records, retention, signatures, exception reporting, and escalation.
- **Clinical boundary:** What must enter the resident health record versus production records.

### 12. Clean and sanitize equipment

- **Existing instruction:** Clean, sanitize, inspect, and store equipment under manufacturer and facility procedures.
- **Purpose:** Prevent contamination and prepare for reuse.
- **Expected result:** Equipment passes the documented post-use check.
- **Measurable check:** Complete sanitation check and record damage or maintenance.
- **Risk:** High.
- **Common mistake:** Cleaning visible surfaces only or reassembling soiled/damaged parts.
- **Corrective action:** Remove from service and repeat approved cleaning, sanitation, inspection, or maintenance.
- **Evidence claims:** Food-contact equipment requires effective cleaning and sanitizing; full wash-rinse-sanitize-air-dry cleaning can reduce allergen transfer.
- **Operational judgment:** Cleaning sequence only where manufacturer instructions permit discretion.
- **Facility policy:** Chemicals, concentrations, contact times, verification, storage, and out-of-service rules.
- **Clinical boundary:** Resident-specific allergen precautions.

## Audit conclusion

The seed correctly avoided unsupported numbers and claims, but every step contains at least one proposition that requires authoritative, facility-specific, or qualified review. Steps 1, 4, 6–10, and 12 remain high risk. None can be considered publication-ready merely because an authoritative document has been located.
