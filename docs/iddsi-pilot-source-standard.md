# IDDSI Pilot Source Standard

## Controlled hierarchy

Use the highest applicable source. Lower tiers may add local implementation context but may not contradict or silently broaden higher-tier material.

| Priority | Source category | May support | Cannot support | Verification expectation | Review frequency | Withdrawal or supersession risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Official IDDSI materials | Framework definitions, Level 4 descriptors, named testing methods, implementation considerations | Resident diagnosis, diet prescription, facility compliance, or a claim that a specific recipe passes without testing | Confirm the document is hosted by IDDSI, record version/date/URL, compare against current IDDSI resource listings, and have an IDDSI-competent reviewer verify application | At least annually and whenever IDDSI announces an update | Medium; framework or testing guidance may be revised, translated, clarified, or relocated |
| 2 | United States federal food-safety guidance or regulation | Federal requirements in scope and model food-safety controls | State/local adoption, facility-specific limits, clinical decisions, or IDDSI conformity | Confirm current agency/eCFR source and effective/current date; distinguish regulation from guidance/model code | At least annually and after regulatory or Food Code updates | Medium to high; regulations and model codes change, and Food Code adoption varies |
| 3 | Applicable state or local requirements | Jurisdiction-specific food code, licensing, temperature, sanitation, and documentation requirements | Requirements outside the named jurisdiction | Confirm current official government publication, applicability to facility type/location, effective date, and amendments | At least annually and on jurisdiction update | High when location is unknown or local adoption changes |
| 4 | Recognized professional organizations | Scope-of-practice context, consensus practice, competencies, implementation guidance | Binding law, resident-specific orders, or universal facility policy | Verify publisher, current status, disclosed methodology, and fit to the precise claim | Every 12–24 months or on update | Medium |
| 5 | Manufacturer documentation | Operation, capacity, assembly, cleaning, maintenance, and safety for the exact equipment/model | Clinical suitability, IDDSI conformity by itself, other models, or local sanitation policy | Match exact manufacturer/model/manual revision; verify official source and current service bulletin status | On equipment/manual change and at least annually | High if equipment/model is not identified |
| 6 | Facility policies and procedures | Local order source, approved recipes, adopted limits, allergen controls, corrective actions, records, escalation, and role ownership | Universal standards or application to another facility | Named policy owner, identifier, version/effective date, approval, and applicability to the specific site | At policy review date and at least annually | High; local controls can change quickly |
| 7 | Qualified interdisciplinary professional review | Application of evidence to operational, nutrition, swallowing, diet-order, or facility-policy boundaries | Replacement for a source, authorization outside reviewer scope, or undocumented personal preference | Record reviewer identity, role, credentials, scope, conflict check, review date, and decision | At material revision and scheduled review | Medium; credentials, role, or care standards may change |
| 8 | Documented direct professional experience | Clearly labeled operational observations, feasibility, failure modes, and local workflow refinements | Standards, regulation, clinical outcomes, numerical limits, or general safety claims | Identify practitioner, setting, dates, method, conflicts, and corroboration | At least annually | High; context-specific and vulnerable to recall/selection bias |
| 9 | Editorial judgment | Organization, wording, sequence, readability, and explicitly labeled conservative choices | Factual, clinical, regulatory, safety, performance, or conformity claims | Named editor, rationale, date, and clear separation from evidence | Every material revision | High if mistaken for evidence |

## Excluded authoritative evidence

Affiliate content, retailer copy, generic blogs, anonymous summaries, AI output, search-result snippets, and unsourced marketing claims are not authoritative sources. They may be used only to discover a primary source, which must then be independently opened and recorded.

## Pilot verification rules

- A URL is not verification.
- A source record and each claim link have separate status and confidence.
- “Verified” requires a named qualified human verifier and date.
- One source may support multiple claims only when each link states the precise support and limitation.
- Workflow-level evidence cannot satisfy a high-risk step-level gate.
- Facility policy must be entered as a real, versioned facility document; placeholders do not count as sources.
- State/local material must not be selected until the facility jurisdiction is known.
- Manufacturer material must not be selected until exact equipment is known.
- Archived, withdrawn, superseded, or inaccessible material must be labeled and cannot satisfy a current publication gate.

## Source review triggers

Review immediately when:

- IDDSI changes a framework definition, test, implementation note, or resource status;
- a regulation, Food Code edition/supplement, or jurisdictional adoption changes;
- a facility policy, recipe, order workflow, allergen control, or documentation form changes;
- equipment, model, attachment, chemical, or manufacturer instruction changes;
- a failed test, safety event, complaint, survey finding, or corrective action challenges a mapped claim;
- the workflow is adapted to a different jurisdiction, population, facility type, or production system.

## Current collection boundary

The pilot records current primary sources that can be verified bibliographically. They remain `draft` in the application because no qualified human verifier has attested to their application. No state/local source, facility policy, manufacturer manual, or professional-experience source is entered without the missing jurisdiction, facility document, equipment identity, or practitioner record.
