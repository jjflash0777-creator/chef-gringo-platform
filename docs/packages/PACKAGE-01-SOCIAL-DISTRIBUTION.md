## Learn — Temperature carryover
Destination: /learn/temperature-carryover

### Facebook
**Hook:** A temperature reading is a snapshot, not the end of the cooking process.

When food leaves the heat, the outside can still be much hotter than the center. That stored heat keeps moving inward, which is why the center temperature can continue to rise while the food rests.

The useful habit: measure the part you actually care about, understand where the coldest zone is likely to be, and remember that one reading does not describe the entire food.

Read the full guide: /learn/temperature-carryover

### Instagram
**Carousel concept:** "Why the temperature keeps rising after the heat stops"
1. The surface is hotter than the center.
2. Heat keeps moving inward after cooking stops.
3. Your probe only measures the area around its sensor.
4. Thick or irregular foods may need more than one check.
5. Instant-read and leave-in probes solve different jobs.

Caption CTA: Save this for your next roast, steak, or large batch cook. Full guide at Chef Gringo.

### TikTok
**Hook:** "You pulled the food off the heat. So why is the center temperature still going up?"
**Script:** The outside is hotter than the center. Once you remove the heat source, that stored heat keeps moving inward. That is carryover cooking. And it is also why probe placement matters: the thermometer is reading one location, not the whole food. For repeated spot checks, use an instant-read. If you need to watch a temperature change over time, that is a different job for a leave-in probe.

## Solve — Suspicious thermometer reading
Destination: /services/thermometer-troubleshooting

### Facebook
**Hook:** Before throwing away a thermometer because the reading looks wrong, separate instrument failure from measurement failure.

Check the location of the probe, allow the sensor time to respond, inspect the probe and cable, then follow the manufacturer's calibration/verification procedure. A reading can be real and still be the wrong reading for the question you meant to answer.

Troubleshooting sequence: /services/thermometer-troubleshooting

### Instagram
**Carousel concept:** "Thermometer reading looks wrong? Check these first."
1. Repeat the measurement.
2. Change probe location.
3. Allow the sensor to stabilize.
4. Inspect the probe/cable/connector.
5. Verify using the maker's calibration procedure.
6. Remove questionable equipment from service according to your operation's policy.

### Pinterest
**Pin title:** Kitchen Thermometer Troubleshooting Checklist
**Description:** A practical sequence for checking probe placement, response time, damaged probes, and calibration before replacing the instrument.
**Destination:** /services/thermometer-troubleshooting

### TikTok
**Hook:** "Your thermometer says something impossible. Don't throw it away yet."
**Script:** First, repeat the reading somewhere else. Second, make sure the sensor is not touching bone, a pan, or a heating element. Third, give the thermometer enough time to respond. Then inspect the probe and follow the manufacturer's calibration procedure. You are trying to answer one question: is the instrument wrong, or are we measuring the wrong place?

Destination: /business/food-truck-temperature-kit

## Manage — Five-minute temperature-control check
Destination: /culinary-director-tools/temperature-control-check

### Facebook
**Hook:** Managers: a full temperature log does not automatically mean you have a working temperature-control system.

Five-minute check:
- Are the thermometers where the work happens?
- Is calibration/verification current?
- Do refrigeration readings make sense?
- Are staff documenting real exceptions and corrective action?
- Does the next shift know what is wrong?
