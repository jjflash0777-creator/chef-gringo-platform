# Chef Gringo Knowledge Constitution

Status: governing draft for adoption before the Knowledge Core implementation sprint.

## Purpose

Chef Gringo exists to help people make safer, clearer, and more effective culinary and hospitality decisions. Its knowledge system is not a volume-content engine, merchant catalog, or affiliate-ranking mechanism. It is a governed body of practical knowledge whose records are connected, reviewable, attributable, and useful in a real operating context.

These rules apply to products, problems, jobs, workflows, workflow steps, guides, reviews, comparisons, personas, environments, evidence, sources, and recommendations.

## Permanent principles

1. Trust precedes monetization.
2. A real problem precedes a product.
3. A job and outcome precede a recommendation.
4. Evidence precedes confidence.
5. Context precedes generalization.
6. Drawbacks and exclusions are part of the answer.
7. One canonical record is the source of truth for each entity.
8. Every published claim is attributable and reviewable.
9. Every important change is traceable.
10. Correcting knowledge is a duty, not an embarrassment.

## Required knowledge questions

Every publishable knowledge record must answer, directly or through required relationships:

1. What problem does this solve?
2. Who is it for?
3. In what environment is it used?
4. What job does it help complete?
5. What evidence supports it?
6. What outcome should result?
7. What should the user do next?
8. Who reviewed it?
9. When was it last verified?
10. What related records should it connect to?

A record that cannot answer a question must either document why the question is not applicable or remain unpublished.

## Canonical entity rules

### Products

- One product/model has one canonical product record.
- Vendor offers, prices, commissions, and availability are separate records.
- Product facts must distinguish manufacturer claims, third-party evidence, and Chef Gringo operational experience.
- “Best for” requires both a target context and a rationale.
- “Not recommended for” is mandatory whenever foreseeable misuse or poor fit exists.
- A product cannot be ranked because of commission, inventory, sponsorship, free samples, or partner pressure.

### Problems

- A problem is a user-observable or operator-observable condition, not a product category.
- Problem statements use plain language and describe the cost, risk, or friction being experienced.
- Duplicate phrasings of the same problem must resolve to one canonical record with aliases, not multiple competing concepts.
- Problems must connect to jobs, workflows, environments, personas, and measurable outcomes where relevant.

### Jobs to Be Done

- A job describes progress a person or team is trying to make under specific circumstances.
- A job is outcome-oriented and vendor-neutral.
- A job must not be reduced to “buy a product.”
- Jobs may connect to multiple workflows and solutions.

### Workflows and workflow steps

- A workflow is an ordered operational method for completing a job.
- Every workflow names its entry conditions, required inputs, intended outcome, environment, responsible role, safety/compliance boundaries, and next action.
- Steps are atomic, ordered, testable, and written as actions.
- Critical control points, common mistakes, corrective actions, and measurable checks belong to the relevant step.
- Product recommendations may support steps but may not replace the workflow.

### Personas

- Personas represent material differences in goals, authority, skill, constraints, or risk—not marketing stereotypes.
- A persona must explain needs and constraints.
- Recommendations must state why a fit differs by persona.

### Environments

- Environments represent operational conditions that change the advice: volume, staffing, resident/patient needs, regulations, equipment, service model, sanitation, and risk.
- Environmental assumptions must be explicit.
- Advice cannot be generalized from a home kitchen to healthcare or commercial service without evidence.

### Guides

- A guide solves a defined decision problem.
- It must name the audience, environment, job, criteria, evidence standard, and next action.
- Products referenced by a guide come from canonical product records.
- Rankings require a declared method and contextual rationale.

### Reviews

- A review distinguishes hands-on testing, operational use, expert inspection, document review, and third-party evidence.
- Testing methods, duration, conditions, limitations, conflicts, and disclosures are mandatory.
- A score cannot substitute for a verdict and rationale.
- Reviews must state who should and should not choose the product.

### Comparisons

- A comparison names the decision context and criteria before comparing candidates.
- Criteria weights must reflect the job and environment, not universal preference.
- Candidates must be comparable or the mismatch must be explained.
- The result must permit “neither” when no candidate meets the need.

### Recommendations

- A recommendation is a decision with a rationale, context, evidence set, confidence, exclusions, and next action.
- Recommendation logic must be explainable without exposing confidential internal notes.
- A recommendation can be “do nothing,” “change the workflow,” “seek specialist review,” or “do not buy.”
- Recommendations expire when their evidence, availability, safety, regulation, or operating assumptions become stale.

## Trust-before-monetization rules

- Editorial inclusion, exclusion, ranking, confidence, and wording are decided before merchant selection.
- Commission value is never an editorial input.
- Commercial staff may correct merchant facts but cannot alter editorial conclusions.
- Affiliate links are attached only after a recommendation is approved.
- A product remains eligible for recommendation when no affiliate relationship exists.
- The absence or termination of a commercial relationship cannot erase editorial history.
- Paid placements, if ever introduced, must be visually and structurally separate from recommendations and cannot use recommendation labels.

## Affiliate independence rules

- Every affiliate relationship must be disclosed where a user can act on it.
- The disclosure must be plain, proximate, and understandable.
- Free samples, travel, consulting, sponsorships, and other material relationships count as conflicts and must be recorded.
- The reviewer and approver must be able to reject a partner product without commercial retaliation.
- Affiliate conversion data may improve usability but may not raise recommendation confidence.
- Merchant availability may determine which purchase links are shown, not which product is editorially preferred.

## Evidence standards

### Evidence classes

1. **Authoritative**: current standards, regulations, official clinical/safety guidance, certification records.
2. **Primary technical**: manufacturer manuals, specifications, test reports, warranty/service documentation.
3. **Independent empirical**: credible controlled testing or published research.
4. **Operational**: documented use in a stated environment with method, duration, operator, and limitations.
5. **Expert synthesis**: qualified analysis that cites underlying sources.
6. **Anecdotal**: individual experience without a replicable method.
7. **Commercial claim**: vendor/manufacturer marketing not independently verified.

Rules:

- Safety, clinical, regulatory, allergen, texture-modification, and compliance claims require authoritative or appropriately qualified evidence.
- Commercial claims are labeled and never treated as independent proof.
- Operational evidence records who observed what, where, for how long, and under which conditions.
- Conflicting evidence is retained and explained.
- Absence of evidence is never rewritten as evidence of absence.

## Source attribution requirements

Every material factual claim must connect to a source or a documented direct observation.

Each source record must include:

- title;
- author or responsible organization;
- publisher;
- source type;
- stable URL or document identifier when available;
- publication/revision date when available;
- date accessed;
- applicable jurisdiction or environment;
- relevant excerpt/page/section locator;
- rights or usage note when required;
- status: active, superseded, withdrawn, or inaccessible.

Sources must be cited at claim level for high-risk knowledge. Link lists without claim mapping are insufficient.

## Recommendation confidence standards

Confidence describes the strength and applicability of the recommendation, not enthusiasm.

- **Insufficient**: evidence or context is too weak to recommend; publish only as an identified gap.
- **Low**: plausible fit supported by limited or indirect evidence; prominently qualify.
- **Moderate**: multiple relevant sources or credible operational evidence; material limitations remain.
- **High**: converging authoritative/empirical and operational evidence in the target context; current verification.

Confidence must consider:

- evidence quality and independence;
- recency;
- directness to the persona/environment/job;
- consistency across sources;
- known limitations;
- consequence of error;
- reviewer expertise.

High-risk advice requires a higher evidence threshold. Confidence may be lowered by uncertainty but never raised by popularity, brand reputation alone, or affiliate performance.

## Editorial review requirements

- Author and reviewer identities are recorded.
- High-risk records require a reviewer with relevant competence.
- The reviewer confirms the ten required knowledge questions.
- The reviewer checks sources, conflicts, exclusions, language, and actionable next steps.
- The approver confirms all quality gates; author self-approval is prohibited for high-risk records.
- Review decisions record approved, changes requested, rejected, or withdrawn with a reason.
- Published records have a verification owner and review-by date.

## Revision-history requirements

- Published knowledge is never silently overwritten.
- Every material revision records the actor, timestamp, reason, fields changed, source changes, previous version, and new version.
- Users must be able to identify the current version and last verification date.
- Corrections affecting safety or outcomes receive a corrective-action record and, when appropriate, a visible notice.
- Superseded versions remain internally available for audit.
- Retractions preserve the record and explain why it is no longer reliable.

## Quality gates

A record may be published only when:

1. Identity and canonical uniqueness are resolved.
2. The problem, persona, environment, job, outcome, and next action are present or explicitly not applicable.
3. Material claims have valid sources.
4. Evidence quality and confidence are assigned using the rubric.
5. Benefits, drawbacks, exclusions, and uncertainty are stated.
6. Related canonical records are linked.
7. Affiliate and conflict disclosures are complete.
8. A qualified reviewer has approved it.
9. The last-verified and review-by dates are present.
10. Required safety/compliance review is complete.
11. Common mistakes and corrective actions are present for operational workflows.
12. Measurable outcome or verification criteria are defined.
13. Audit and revision records are created atomically with publication.

Publication must fail closed when a required gate is unmet.

## Rules preventing shallow or unsupported content

- Do not publish listicles whose criteria are undefined.
- Do not restate manufacturer copy as independent expertise.
- Do not use “best,” “professional,” “safe,” “compliant,” or “evidence-based” without scoped support.
- Do not create a new record merely to target a keyword.
- Do not conceal uncertainty behind confident prose.
- Do not present a static price as durable product truth.
- Do not recommend equipment before considering workflow, training, maintenance, and service.
- Do not infer healthcare, IDDSI, allergen, food-safety, or regulatory suitability from general product features.
- Do not allow AI-generated text to become evidence.
- Do not publish AI-generated synthesis without human source verification and editorial approval.
- Do not duplicate canonical facts across guides, reviews, and comparisons.
- Do not convert correlation, popularity, ratings, or sales into causation or fitness.

## AI use rules

- AI may assist discovery, extraction, classification, drafting, and consistency checks.
- AI output is never a source.
- AI recommendations must retrieve governed records, preserve citations, expose confidence and limitations, and produce a human-understandable rationale.
- High-risk outputs must defer to qualified professionals and current authoritative guidance.
- AI may not override a withdrawn source, an editorial restriction, or an affiliate-independence rule.

## Governance and enforcement

- Schema constraints, API validation, admin quality gates, tests, and editorial policy must reinforce one another.
- Policy-only controls are insufficient for publication-critical rules.
- Exceptions require a named owner, documented rationale, expiration date, and audit event.
- This Constitution is versioned. Changes require editorial and technical review because they affect both content and architecture.
