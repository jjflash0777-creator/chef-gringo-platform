# Content Standards

## Originality

Every public resource must be original or appropriately licensed. Writers may research common problems and authoritative public rules, but must not reproduce proprietary SOPs, manuals, menus, courseware, certification questions, or competitor templates.

## Evidence and Claims

- Link to primary authorities for regulations, safety, certifications, and technical claims.
- Date material that can change.
- Distinguish observation, professional opinion, and verified fact.
- Never invent credentials, outcomes, statistics, quotes, endorsements, or user results.
- Describe preview features as previews.

## Educational Structure

State the audience, purpose, prerequisites, expected outcome, practical steps, common failure modes, safety or regulatory limits, and a next action. Advanced content should not hide behind unnecessary jargon.

## Reviews and Recommendations

Disclose evaluation criteria, testing limitations, material relationships, and who a product fits or does not fit. No pay-to-win rankings. Total cost and operational fit matter more than feature volume.

## Regulated and High-Stakes Topics

Food safety, alcohol service, employment, finance, legal compliance, healthcare dining, allergies, swallowing, and certifications require clear boundaries and current primary sources. Chef Gringo does not replace qualified professional advice or authoritative certification.

## Maintenance

Each substantial resource should have an owner, review date, source record, and change history. Retire material that can no longer be supported.
